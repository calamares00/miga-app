## 📚 Books and recipes

Each book is a recipe collection with its own cover: your mum's, a friend's or one per theme (the best brownies deserve a book of their own). New recipes are saved in the book you have open.

- When you **create or edit a book** you see its cover as it will look. The photo can come from the gallery, the camera or one of its recipes; without a photo, choose its **colour** and a **picture**. Add an **author** and a **dedication** too: they show when you open the book, on its cover and in the book's PDF.
- In **Books**, the filters at the top (All, My books, Packs, Server) show each kind of book. You can also **swipe left or right** to move from one to the next.
- Long-press a book to **select it**, then tap others to add them. At the top you have Edit, Share and Delete (after deleting you have a few seconds to **Undo**), and under **More actions**, **Pin to top**: pinned books come first, with a pin on their cover. Inside a book you can also pin it from its menu (⋮).
- Inside a book, recipes are grouped by **category**. Tap a category to collapse or expand it; the menu (⋮) collapses or expands them all.
- When a recipe is open, **swipe left or right** to go to the next or previous recipe of the book.
- Long-press a recipe to **select several**. At the top you can edit it (if only one), share or delete them (with **Undo** for a few seconds), and under **More actions** change their category, difficulty, servings or source at once, mark them as favourites, move or copy them to another book, add their ingredients to the shopping list or **polish them** ✨ all in one go.
- In each recipe, **My notes** keeps dated personal notes ("next time, less salt"). They do not change the recipe and go into backups.
- **Search** looks through every book by name or ingredient. The filter button narrows it down by time, difficulty, category, equipment, tags, ingredients or origin, and also by **allergens** ("No eggs", "No gluten ingredients"…) or **for coeliacs**. At the bottom, **Show N recipes** says how many are left. Active filters show up as chips you can remove one by one. The bookmark **saves the search** under a name, and before you type your saved and recent searches are at hand. **Favourites** works the same, with your favourites only.
- **Source**: if it is a link, tap it to open the web page; the copy button next to it copies the address.
- **What can I cook?** (in Search): type the ingredients you have and see the recipes that use them, those needing fewest extra things first, with what is missing. Salt, water, oil and pepper are taken for granted.

## ✍️ Writing a recipe

- **Ingredients**: as you type the name, the app suggests the ones you have used before. From each ingredient's menu (⋮) you can mark it as **optional** (parsley to garnish, for example): it shows "(optional)" without changing its name.
- **Tabs** at the top (Basics, Ingredients, Steps, More) jump to each part of the recipe.
- **Ingredients**: they look as in the recipe; tap one to edit its name, amount and unit. To add one, type "200 g potatoes" in the box below and Miga splits amount, unit and name.
- **Paste a list**: paste several ingredients or steps at once (or use **Paste list**) and they are split one per line, without bullets or numbers; ingredients are split into quantity, unit and name.
- **Reorder**: drag the handle (⋮⋮) of an ingredient or step to move it.
- **Repeated recipes**: if you already have a recipe with the same name, you are asked before saving.
- **Sub-recipes**: split the recipe into parts (for example, "For the sauce"), each with its own ingredients and steps.
- **Steps and notes** support formatting: while you type, a bar offers **bold**, *italics*, strikethrough and lists. Pressing Enter in a list starts the next item.
- **Dictation**: tap the microphone to dictate the steps instead of typing them.
- **Origin**: where the recipe comes from (Mexico, Córdoba, Thai cuisine…). The flag appears on its own when the country is recognised; tap the globe or the flag to choose it.
- **Servings**: in the recipe, change the servings and the quantities are recalculated.
- **Photos**: when adding a photo you can crop it, rotate it and **enhance it** ✨ (light, colour and sharpness); it is saved at just the right size so it does not take up more space than needed.
- **Polish the recipe** ✨: from the recipe's menu (⋮), the assistant tidies up the wording, normalises the ingredients and highlights times and temperatures in bold. You see the before and after and decide whether to apply it; nothing is saved without your say-so.

## 📥 Adding recipes from other sources

From a book, tap **New recipe** and choose where it comes from:

- **Write it yourself**: an empty recipe for you to fill in.
- **From photos** ✨: take photos or pick up to 20 from the gallery, of one recipe or several. Miga works out which photos belong to the same recipe (two pages, or the recipe and a photo of the dish) and which are different recipes. If it is a single one, the editor opens already filled in for you to check; if there are several, they are saved one by one and you see the result in a list. The assistant reads the text and looks for the photo of the dish: it checks that it really is the finished dish, crops it without text or margins and makes the one showing the whole dish the main photo.
- **From a web page**: paste the link to a recipe. Most recipe websites include it in a standard format (schema.org) that Miga reads directly, with its photo, times and servings, **without the assistant**. If the assistant is set up, it then **improves** it ✨ like "Polish the recipe" (wording, units, times in bold) and tells you what changed, with a button to **undo the improvement**. If the site does not use that format, the assistant reads it.
- **From Instagram**: paste the link to a post or reel, or tap **Share** in Instagram and choose Miga (it asks which book to save it in). If the caption has the ingredients and the steps under their headings ("Ingredients", "Steps"…), Miga reads them **without the assistant**, with the photo and the author in the notes; otherwise the assistant reads it. If it is a video and the text has no recipe, the assistant **listens to the video**: Gemini hears the audio, and OpenAI, Groq or Mistral transcribe it first (not possible with Claude, DeepSeek or OpenRouter). Check the quantities: the recipe gets a note reminding you. Private posts cannot be imported.
- **From YouTube**: paste the link to a video or a Short, or tap **Share** in YouTube and choose Miga. If the description has the recipe, Miga reads it **without the assistant**; if it links to the author's website, it takes it from there. Otherwise **Gemini watches the video** (what is said and what is written on screen); the other assistants can only read the description or the linked website. Check the quantities.
- **From a Miga file**: imports recipes exported from Miga (.miga, .zip or JSON).
- **Suggest a recipe** ✨: type the name of a dish and choose from the suggestions.

Options marked ✨ are done by the kitchen assistant and only appear once it is set up.

## ⚠️ Allergens and intolerances

From the ingredients, and without the assistant, Miga works out the **14 allergens** EU law requires to be declared (gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soy, milk, tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphites, lupin and molluscs) and **lactose**.

- In the recipe, an **Allergens** line shows only the ones it has: red for what it **contains**; amber with "!" for what it **may contain** depending on the product you buy (stock cubes, sausages, spice mixes, chocolate…). Tap it to see all of them, which ingredient brings each one and advice for coeliacs.
- In the ingredient list, ⚠ marks those that may bring an allergen depending on the product (with "Only mine", only your allergens, red when they surely have them; with "No", none except gluten if you cook for coeliacs). Tap it to see which one.
- **Gluten**: "No ingredients with gluten" or "Suitable for coeliacs if…" (for example, if you buy the sausage and stock certified gluten-free and cook without cross-contact). Miga never says just "gluten-free": that is a legal claim that needs the product to be tested. Oats count as a cereal with gluten unless certified. Buckwheat, maize, rice or chickpea flour and lentils have no gluten but may carry traces: the coeliac card reminds you to buy them certified.
- If you use a certified product, mark it in the editor from the ingredient's menu (⋮): **Gluten-free product (certified)** or **Lactose-free product**. It is added to the name, so it also shows in the shopping list.
- In **Settings → Health** you choose what recipes show: **All** allergens (as before), **Only mine** or **No**. Allergens are always in the recipe menu (⋮) → **Allergens**.
- **My allergies and intolerances**: mark yours and the recipe says "Has egg" or "None of your allergens". You can ask for a **warning in recipe lists** and for every search to start with the "No…" filter on.
- **I cook for coeliacs**: each recipe says whether it has ingredients with gluten and what to buy certified, even if you hide allergens. You can also always search only recipes for coeliacs.
- It is **for guidance only**: check each product's label and ask your doctor or dietitian.

## 🩺 Carbohydrates and portion plan (diabetes)

For those who count carbohydrate portions. Everything is in **Settings → Health** and starts off.

- **Count carbohydrate portions**: each recipe shows how many portions it has per serving, worked out from its ingredients and amounts, without the assistant. Tap the line to see what each ingredient brings, what could not be counted (for example "flour to coat" with no amount) and what has hardly any carbohydrate.
- **Grams per portion**: 10 by default (1 portion = 10 g in Spain; US carb choices are 15 g). Use the one your care team gave you; at 0 only grams are shown.
- **My portion plan**: copy your care team's plan, with the portions of each group (milk, protein, vegetables, starches, fruit and fat) at each meal. There is a 1,500 kcal example to start with, but always use yours.
- With a plan, each recipe shows its portions by group, and **Fit to my meal** tells you how much to take for that meal and what to add to complete it (bread, fruit, salad…) in kitchen measures, or what is over. If the recipe does not fit that meal (a burger when your plan only has milk), it says so and which meals it fits.
- It counts the part that is eaten (no bones or shells), oil to fry or confit as 3 tablespoons per serving at most, and sweets as 1 serving at most.
- Values come from USDA FoodData Central checked against BEDCA (AESAN), raw. It is **for guidance only**: it does not replace your care team's advice.

## 🍳 Cooking mode

In a recipe with steps, tap **Cooking mode** to see them one at a time in full screen. The sun icon chooses whether the screen **stays on** (remembered for next time). Tick off the ingredients as you use them.

- Times mentioned in a step ("simmer for 10 minutes") become one-tap **timers**, and the clock icon adds others with any name and minutes ("Rice", 18). You can have **several at once** and they keep counting after you close cooking mode or leave the app: when one ends, an alert sounds. For it to ring on time with the screen off, Android may ask you to allow **exact alarms**.
- The app can **read each step aloud**. The voice is chosen in Settings → Voice and dictation.
- With the **voice command** (microphone) you can say "next", "previous", "repeat" or "timer".
- Turn on **hands-free** (the speaking-person icon) so it listens all the time: say "next" with your hands in the dough and it moves on without touching the phone. It stops listening while it reads a step aloud; to turn it off, tap the icon again.

## 🛒 Shopping list

- Add items by typing, **dictating** or **scanning the barcode**. From a recipe you can add all its ingredients at once, and the explore button opens the product **catalogue** and the Open Food Facts search.
- Items are grouped by category and sorted by **your supermarket's aisles**.
- You can have **several lists** and save **templates** for the weekly shop. On tablets and large screens the lists show on the left, and Settings shows each section next to its list.
- **Swipe** an item right to tick it and left to remove it.
- **Shop mode** keeps only what you need while shopping and stops the screen from turning off.
- The **product sheet** (Open Food Facts) shows Nutri-Score, NOVA, Eco-Score, allergens, additives and an indicative score.
- Share the list as text or with a **QR code**, and use the **widget** to add things without opening the app.

## ✨ Ideas {ai}

Tap ✨ at the top of **Library** to ask for suggestions based on your recipes.

- Tap a quick idea ("What for breakfast?", weekly menu…) or combine options at the bottom: meal, style (healthy, quick, vegetarian, seasonal…), kind of dish and equipment, even a specific model such as "Thermomix TM31".
- You can also ask anything about cooking: techniques, substitutions, storage…
- Suggested recipes appear by name and open with a tap. You can add their ingredients to the shopping list, and from the **dishes you could add** create a new one in the book you choose.
- The assistant only talks about cooking and food, and never changes anything in the app.

## 📤 Sharing

**Share** is at the top of each recipe, in a book's menu (⋮) and at the top when recipes or books are selected. Choose how:

- **Send to someone**: a .miga file that opens with Miga on the other phone. It leaves out your favourites, ratings and notes.
- **Create link**: an encrypted link for WhatsApp, email…, which expires after 7 days.
- **PDF**: to read, print or send. It cannot be imported back into Miga.
- **Image** (one recipe): the whole recipe in one picture (photo, ingredients and steps), ready for Instagram or WhatsApp.
- **Text** (one recipe): to paste into a message.
- **Save a copy (.zip)**: with the photos, to import it into Miga later, on this phone or another.

Whoever receives a recipe chooses the book to keep it in; a book arrives as a new book or, if they already had it, is updated. Either way they see "Shared by" with the name you set in Settings → Your name.

## 👤 Your name

Signs what you share: shared shopping lists and the books and recipes you send. Whoever receives them sees "Shared by …".

## 🎨 Appearance

Light, dark or system theme, accent colour, app language and **photo frame**: choose one (Polaroid, gallery, vignette…) and every photo of your recipes matches.

## 🔒 Security

Turn on the **biometric lock** so the app asks for your fingerprint, face or PIN when it opens. If the phone has none set up, add one in Android's settings first.

## 🎙️ Voice and dictation

- **Dictation language**: the language used to recognise what you dictate (shopping list, recipe steps and cooking mode).
- **Voice**: the one that reads the steps aloud in cooking mode. Tap **Test voice** to hear it.

## 🗂️ Manage content

The lists of recipe categories, equipment, tags, ingredients and products, and product categories.

- Search, filter what is unused and sort by name or by use. Tap "N recipes" to see the recipes that use it.
- Renaming to a name that already exists **merges** the two, and the affected recipes are updated (in synced books too).
- **Clean up content** finds duplicates, names to unify and your own unused items, and tidies them up in one go. With the assistant on it is even sharper, and "Undo" is always there, just in case.

## 💾 Backup

- **Export the whole app** saves books, recipes and photos in one file, to restore it later on this phone or another.
- The backup can be **password-protected**: it is encrypted with AES-256 and only opens with that password. Miga does not store it anywhere, so if you forget it there is no way to recover the backup.
- **Import backup** restores a backup (ZIP, JSON or password-protected); if you like, it can delete what you have first.
- **Import recipes or a book** adds what a Miga file holds: one recipe, a selection or a whole book. For a book, it asks whether to add its recipes to one of your books or create it as a new book.
- While exporting or importing, Miga shows the progress (also in a notification, so you can keep using the app).

## 👋 Tour and permissions

The **welcome tour** sums up what you can do with Miga: recipes, cooking, tips, shopping, health and the assistant. To see it again: **Settings › Help › See now**.

Miga only asks for permissions when it needs them, and all of them are optional:
- **Notifications**: alerts from timers and when the assistant finishes a task.
- **Alarms on time**: timers go off on time even with the screen off.
- **Microphone**: dictating recipes and the shopping list, and hands-free cooking mode.
- **Camera**: taking photos of your dishes and recipes, and scanning barcodes and QR codes.

You can change them at any time in Android's settings.

## 📊 Statistics

Figures about your recipes: recipes per book, category and difficulty, favourites, best rated and most cooked, times, most used ingredients and tags, world cuisines and recipes to complete (no photo, no steps…). Tap a figure to see its recipes. At the bottom you can see how much space Miga takes on the phone, with options to clear the cache and delete photos no recipe uses any more.

## 💡 Cooking tips

Short tips to cook better: techniques, storing and freezing, using leftovers, substitutions, food safety, baking, saving time and kitchen tools. Find them under the 💡 icon in **Books**; they work offline.

- Tap a tip to read it in full; the ⭐ keeps it in **Saved**, and you can share it too.
- Swipe left or right to move between categories, or search by words.
- **Tip of the day**: shown at the top of Books. The ✕ hides it until tomorrow, and the ⋮ menu in Tips turns it off altogether.
- In a recipe, if any tip helps with its ingredients or steps (aubergine or pasta, say), you'll see **💡 Tips for this recipe** between the ingredients and the method.

## 🔄 Sync

To share books and shopping lists with your family with full editing, connect Miga to a server you host yourself (miga-server). In **Manage connections** you add the server and namespace, and invite others with a QR code. Your data only goes to your server.

## 📦 Recipe packs

- **Explore catalogue**: books ready to install. A pack is installed as one more book; its recipes cannot be edited, but you can select them and copy them to one of your books.
- **Contribute to the install counter**: installing a pack from the official catalogue adds one anonymous install, to show which ones people like most. You can turn it off.
- **Alternative catalogue**: use another catalogue by entering its URL or a GitHub user/repo.

## 🤖 Kitchen assistant {ai}

Features marked ✨ are done by the kitchen assistant, which uses artificial intelligence (AI). They are optional and use **your own key** for Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude, OpenAI, DeepSeek (very low cost; only its Flash models read photos), Mistral (European), Groq (very fast, with a free tier) or OpenRouter (which has free models; with **Connect with OpenRouter** you log in in the browser, authorise Miga and the key is created and saved for you). With **OpenAI-compatible** you can use any other service with that API, such as OpenCode Zen, or a model on your own computer with Ollama or LM Studio: enter its address (for example `http://192.168.1.10:11434/v1`) and, if it asks for one, the key. Every provider is set up the same way: key, model and, where it depends on the model, the **This model reads photos** switch, which says whether photos can be sent to it. The list of models loads on its own from the provider with your key (OpenRouter's from its public catalogue), and those that read photos are tagged **Images**.

- If you add several providers, they are used **in the order you choose**: if one fails, the next one is tried automatically.
- Each provider has a **switch**: turn it off whenever you like without losing its key.
- **Get a key** opens the provider's page where keys are created; copy it there and, back in Miga, you are offered to use it with one tap (Miga only looks at what you copied at that moment and clears it from the clipboard once saved).
- **Test connection** sends a small request with that key and model and tells you whether it works and how long it takes; if the model is set as reading photos, it also tries a photo.
- While it works, the app tells you **what it is doing** ("Reading the text in the photo…", "Going through your recipes…").
- If you leave the app or turn off the screen, the task **keeps going in the background** and a notification shows its progress. When a polish finishes, the notification takes you straight to the result to review it.
- The assistant can also rate how healthy a recipe is, estimate its nutrition and suggest **substitutes** for an ingredient (long-press the ingredient).
- AI-generated content is marked as such and may contain mistakes: check it. If something is inappropriate, use **Report AI content**.
